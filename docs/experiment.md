# Experiment

**Question:** can the familiarity and emotional pull of a favorite scene help a learner move from recognizing scripted dialogue to understanding and speaking in an unfamiliar conversation?

These signals are fixed before any data exists so the result can't be read to fit. The numbers are first guesses; changing one means editing this file in a commit that says why, before looking at the data it would affect.

## Signals

| Brief asks | Signal | "Yes" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Does the learner enjoy repeating it? | Unprompted takes and check-ins | At least 10 takes of a scene over 2 weeks without a reminder; most check-ins 3 or 4 |
| Do they understand more with fewer prompts? | Hints per line and support level | Hints per line in takes 8–10 are at most half of takes 1–3; most lines reach the bare support level |
| Can they respond when a line varies? | Improv passes | At least 70% of swap variations passed within 3 takes of unlocking improv |

## Comparison

The two starter scenes differ on purpose: the **family dinner** is emotional and the **taxi** scene is practical. If the emotional scene does not beat the practical one on unprompted takes and check-ins, the "emotional pull" half of the hypothesis is in doubt.

## Caveat

The first learner also designed the app. Treat a result as a strong hint, not proof. Opt-in research stats from other learners are the way past this.
